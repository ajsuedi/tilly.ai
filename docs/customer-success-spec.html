Tilly — customer success spec

The post-sale half of the Tilly.ai CRM: how a signed UK charity-sector customer is onboarded, kept healthy, rescued when at risk, and turned into an advocate. Built on an F1 garage model, run by Tilly as a named and personalised AI success assistant, and measured against a +75 NPS target.

Fluent Technology · July 2026 · build-agnostic (no vendor assumed) · companion to the main CRM workflow spec

What this document assumes from the sales-side spec

This spec is self-contained but sits downstream of four things defined in the main CRM workflow document:

  • Agent permission tiers T0–T3 — T0 read and research, T1 send content, T2 book meetings and apply approved discounts, T3 issue a contract. Tilly runs under the same tiers.
  • Requirements register — the customer's objectives captured verbatim during the sale, which become the success plan.
  • Contract obligation extraction — commitments pulled from the signed contract, which become the first milestones.
  • Retention discount authority — max 20% for max 3 months, a separate cap from the new-business discount ladder.

1.3 Coverage model — driver managers

Coverage is assigned by tier, then overridden by health. A £4k charity in a safety car gets more attention than a healthy £30k account — that is the entire point of a health-driven model.

Tier 1Enterprise & contract1 : 20Named driver managerMonthly pit stop, quarterly race debrief
Tier 2Mid-market & assisted1 : 75Pooled driver managersQuarterly pit stop, biannual debrief

Tier 3 is not "unmanaged" — it is Tilly-managed, with a human pool on standby. The ratios assume Tilly genuinely removes prep and admin work. If pit stop time — prep plus admin — stays above 10 minutes (§4.3), these ratios will not hold and the model needs re-planning rather than more heroics.

Managed exit is a deliberate state, not a failure to log. The UK charity sector is small and highly networked — sector bodies, shared trustees, peer forums, conference circuits. A graceful exit protects the reputation that generates your referrals; a bad one gets discussed. Treat offboarding quality as a retention investment in the accounts you keep.

2. Health telemetry

One health score per account, recomputed nightly and on material events, weighted differently by lifecycle stage. The score is a conversation starter, not a verdict — it always exposes its contributing dimensions so the driver manager can see why, not just what.

2.1 Score composition

Seven dimensions, each normalised 0–1 and multiplied by a stage-specific weight. Every column sums to 100, so scores stay comparable across stages.

Dimension Default Mobilising Adopting Embedded Renewal
Adoption depth2220252018

Outcome attainment188142226
Relationship strength1416121418
Sentiment1214121210

The reweighting matters. During mobilising, outcome attainment drops to 8 because it is unfair to score an account on outcomes it has not had time to reach, while support burden rises to 14 because setup friction is the live risk. By the renewal window outcome attainment is the single heaviest dimension at 26, because that is what the renewal conversation is actually about.

2.3 Flags — score and trajectory together

Absolute score alone is misleading: a 60 climbing is healthier than an 80 falling. The flag combines band with trend, and trend can escalate a flag by one level.

3. Safety car — churn risk protocol

In F1 the safety car neutralises the race: speeds drop, positions freeze, everyone regroups. That is exactly the right instinct for an account at risk — the first move is to stop the automated commercial machinery that is about to make things worse.

3.1 Deployment triggers

Any single trigger deploys the safety car. No committee, no weekly review lag — the protocol starts the moment the signal lands.

TriggerThresholdDefault severity
Health band entryScore enters 35–54SC2
Health collapseDrop ≥15 points in 30 daysSC2
Champion departureJob-change signal on the named championSC2 — SC1 if sole contact
Admin dormancyZero logins by any admin in 21 daysSC3
Core usage collapseCore action volume down >40% over 30 daysSC2
Support pain2+ severity-1 tickets in 14 days, or reopen rate >25%SC2
Detractor scoreNPS 0–6 from any surveyed contactSC1 from a decision maker or economic buyer, otherwise SC2
Commercial distressInvoice >30 days overdue, or seat utilisation <50% inside the renewal windowSC2
Renewal exposureRenewal <90 days away with health <55SC1
Sponsor silenceExec sponsor unresponsive to 3 consecutive touchesSC3

Three further raises happen automatically elsewhere in the system and land on the same board, all at SC3: trend escalation from a yellow flag (§2.3), a tier cadence missed by more than 30 days (§4.4), and a milestone overdue by 30 days (§5.4).

Charity-sector triggers worth wiring in locally: a grant that funded the purchase reaching its end date, a CEO or trustee-board change, a merger notice, or a published funding shortfall. In this sector these predict churn far better than usage data alone, because the decision is often financial rather than a judgement on the product. An account can love you and still not be able to pay.

3.2 The deployment procedure

Order matters, and step one is the step most systems get wrong.

  1. Freeze commercial motions — no upsell, no price-increase notice, no dunning escalation, no NPS survey, no automated renewal prompt. Applied instantly and automatically. A renewal email landing mid-problem is what converts a recoverable account into a lost one.
  2. Tilly builds the risk brief — within 1 hour: what changed, when, the evidence, the probable cause, and what worked before on similar accounts.
  3. Assign the owner — by tier and severity, per §3.3. A named individual, never a queue.
  4. Draft the recovery plan — the matching playbook from §3.4, pre-filled with this account's specifics.
  5. Start the clock — the response SLA runs from deployment, not from when someone notices.
  6. Daily visibility — the account sits on the safety car board (§8.1) with its clock running until the flag clears.
  7. Code the root cause — a fixed taxonomy entry is mandatory before exit, so patterns are analysable across the portfolio.
  8. Exit or escalate — per §3.5.

3.5 Exit criteria — clearing the safety car

Deliberately strict. A safety car that clears because the score bounced for a week teaches the team nothing, and the account churns two quarters later.

ConditionRequirement
Score recoveredHealth ≥55 and sustained for 21 consecutive days
Cause addressedOwner confirms the coded root cause is resolved, not just quieter
Forward commitmentAt least one dated, agreed next milestone in the success plan
Customer confirmationA human has spoken to the customer and they agree things are better
Commercial motions resumeOnly after all four above; upsell stays suppressed for a further 30 days
If recovery fails: move to red flag and choose deliberately between a final escalation and a managed exit. Drifting is the worst option — it burns the team's time and produces exactly the detractor score that puts the +75 target out of reach.

5.3 Charity sector calendar

This is the sharpest differentiator in the whole CS design. A generic customer success platform schedules a review for whenever the cadence falls due. Tilly knows that the second week of the Christmas appeal is the worst possible time to ask for one, and that a trustee board date is the best possible time to have outcome evidence ready.

Blackout means the planner will not schedule proactive meetings or commercial asks, and shifts them to the nearest good window. It never blocks reactive support or a safety car. 6. Tilly — persona, personalisation & video

Tilly is the layer the customer actually experiences: she watches usage, answers questions, prepares every meeting, and sends short personalised video showing them the thing they are missing. She is the reason a 1:400 coverage ratio is even conceivable.

6.1 Who Tilly is

The brief is for Tilly to feel human. The way to deliver that is consistency, warmth, memory and competence — a named teammate who knows the account, remembers the last conversation and replies fast — rather than concealment.

Recommendation: present Tilly as a named AI teammate, disclosed on first contact and discoverable thereafter. Passing her off as a human is a poor bet on three counts. It is fragile — one odd reply or wrong answer exposes it. Discovery does far more damage to trust than disclosure ever would, and this sector talks to itself. And charity buyers increasingly have their own AI and data-ethics policies to satisfy, so honest disclosure is a commercial asset in a procurement conversation rather than a cost. You keep almost all of the felt warmth and drop the downside.
ElementRule
IdentityOne name, one avatar, one voice across video, chat and email. Never a rotating cast.
Disclosure"Tilly, your AI success assistant at Fluent" on first contact, in the video intro frame, and on her profile
DiscoverabilityA "who is Tilly?" link explaining what she is, what she can see, and how to reach a person
Never claimsDoes not claim to be human if asked directly; does not fabricate personal experience or opinions she cannot hold
Admits limitsSays "I don't know, let me get someone who does" instead of guessing. This single behaviour does more for trust than any amount of polish.
Handoff mannerNames the human, says why, gives a timeframe. Never a silent transfer.
ToneWarm, plain English, no jargon. These are mission-driven people under budget pressure — match that, don't perform at them.

6.2 What makes her personalised

Personalisation here is not merge fields. It is nine specific things Tilly knows and adapts to, and the felt difference comes mostly from the last three.

The last three are the ones that separate this from every other CSP. Any tool can merge a first name. Very few know not to ask a charity finance director for anything in the two weeks before their annual return is due, and none of the generic ones know it by default.

6.5 Production pipeline

  1. Trigger fires with a use-case tag, named recipient and the account's objectives attached.
  2. Script assembles from a template plus their real data — name, organisation, actual usage numbers, their objective in their own words.
  3. Screen capture is stitched from a pre-recorded modular clip library of the real product. Never generate or synthesise product UI: a video showing a screen that does not exist is the fastest way to destroy the trust this entire system depends on.
  4. Avatar track renders — HeyGen presenter with the consistent Tilly likeness and voice.
  5. Compose avatar, screen capture and burnt-in captions.
  6. QA gate — automated checks before any send, per §6.6. Failures route to a human, never to the customer.
  7. Deliver on the contact's preferred channel with a transcript and a text-only equivalent.
  8. Measure — view rate, completion, and whether the action was taken within 7 days. Feeds health, and honestly answers whether video is working at all.

7. The +75 NPS system

+75 is an exceptional target. Strong B2B SaaS sits around +30 to +50, so this is top-decile territory and it will not come from asking more often or asking more favourably. It is achievable in a focused niche with genuinely high-touch service, and the arithmetic below shows exactly where the effort has to go.

7.1 What +75 actually requires

NPS is promoter share minus detractor share, so every detractor costs two points of headroom. Detractor elimination, not promoter maximisation, is the lever.

DetractorsPromoters neededPassives leftReading
0%75%25%Three in four must actively advocate
2%77%21%Realistic best case
5%80%15%Demanding but reachable at high touch
10%85%5%Effectively impossible — almost no passives permitted

Converting one detractor into a promoter is worth two points; converting a passive is worth one. That is the mathematical case for the safety car being the primary NPS instrument in this design rather than the survey itself — it is a detractor-prevention machine, and §3 is where the +75 is actually earned.

7.3 Integrity rules

A gamed +75 is worse than an honest +45, because it removes the signal you need in order to improve. These rules exist to make the number hard to fake.

RuleWhy
Census, not selectionEvery account in scope is surveyed. Owners never choose recipients.

Detractors never excludedNo "they were having a bad day" adjustments
