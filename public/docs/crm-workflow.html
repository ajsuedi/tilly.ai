2. How the pieces connect

The seven-step spine that every record travels, whichever lane it lands in.

  1. Capture — inbound and outbound channels create or match a record. No manual entry.
  2. Enrich — the record is auto-populated with org, person, signal and social data before a human ever sees it.
  3. Score and analyse — Tilly ranks conversion likelihood and overlays competitive position on the specific opportunity.
  4. Route — fit score plus deal complexity decides self-serve e-commerce or enterprise contract path.
  5. Engage — the personalisation layer pushes video, dossiers, proposals and contracts on the best channel.
  6. Escalate only when needed — the agent owns the journey until one of eight defined triggers fires (§11.2).
  7. Compete — outcomes feed the cockpit, where reps and products race on live leaderboards.

4. Predictive scoring model

Two independent scores — fit (who they are, slow-moving) and intent (what they are doing, fast-moving) — blended into a single conversion likelihood. Keeping them separate matters: a perfect-fit charity with no intent needs nurture, a poor-fit org with high intent needs qualifying out. A single blended number hides both cases.

4.1 Fit score features

Scored 0–100. Each feature is normalised to 0–1 then multiplied by its weight. Recomputed on every enrichment refresh, not on every page view.

Income band20Charity Commission / OSCR / CCNI filingsPeak at target band; taper above and below
Funding health15Reserves months, recent grant awards≥6 months reserves = 1.0; <3 months = 0.3

Tech stack compatibility12Site fingerprint, job ads, integrationsKnown-good stack = 1.0; blocking incumbent = 0.2
Cause-area match to ICP12Registered objects, classificationTiered list: core 1.0, adjacent 0.6, out 0.1
Organisation size10Headcount, volunteer countBand curve matched to seat-count sweet spot
Legal structure10Register typeCharity / CIO 1.0, CIC 0.8, contractor 0.7, other 0.4
Geography6Registered address, service areaServiceable nation = 1.0; outside coverage = 0
Total100

4.2 Intent score features

Scored 0–100 with time decay, so a burst of interest three months ago does not read as live interest today. Recomputed on every tracked event and nightly for decay.

4.3 Combining into conversion likelihood

intent_decayed = Σ ( weighti × valuei × 0.5 (days_since / half_lifei) )
raw = (0.40 × fit) + (0.60 × intent_decayed)
likelihood = calibrate(raw)  isotonic regression against closed-won history

Intent is weighted heavier than fit because it moves the near-term forecast. The calibration step is not optional — an uncalibrated 70 means nothing to a rep, whereas a calibrated 70 should close roughly 70% of the time. Track calibration error every month and publish it.

4.4 Bands

Five bands, no gaps and no overlaps across 0–100. Band names carry the F1 metaphor through to the cockpit and drive both agent behaviour and leaderboard multipliers.

4.5 Model governance

The scoring model is a product surface, not a one-off build. It needs an owner, a retraining rhythm and promotion gates.

ControlRule
Cold startShip as a transparent weighted rules model (the tables above). Do not train until ≥200 closed outcomes exist.
Retraining cadenceMonthly, on a rolling 18-month window, 20% time-based holdout
Promotion gateNew model ships only if holdout AUC ≥ 0.72 and beats incumbent by ≥0.02
Drift monitoringPopulation stability index per feature; alert at PSI > 0.2, block auto-retrain at > 0.3
ExplainabilityEvery score exposes its top three contributing features in plain English on the record
Override loopRep can dispute a score with a reason code; disputes are a labelled training input, not just a log
Fairness checkConfirm income band is not proxying to exclude small charities we can profitably serve; review quarterly
Design decision needed: whether small-charity suppression is a deliberate commercial choice or an artefact of the weighting. It changes the income-band curve in §4.1.

5. Competitive analysis engine

Runs per opportunity, not per account — the same charity can be a win on one product and a loss on another. Output is a single verdict plus a recommended commercial move.

5.1 Competitor detection

SignalConfidenceWhat it tells us
Site / DNS tech fingerprintHighIncumbent tooling in active use
Published contract award noticeHighNamed incumbent, value and expiry date
Prospect mentions a vendor in replyHighWho they are actually comparing us against
Job adverts naming a platformMediumCommitted stack, likely switching cost
Review-site activityMediumActive evaluation in progress
Sector inference from peer groupLowPrior only; never drives a price move alone

5.2 Verdict rules

Evaluated in order; first match wins. A price recommendation is only ever produced with a high-confidence competitor signal.

VerdictConditionRecommended play
Loss riskGap on any mandatory requirement, or incumbent tenure >2 years with no renewal window inside 6 monthsNo-bid or reposition to a different product
UndercutRequirement match ≥80% and our cost-to-serve ≤70% of competitor list price and high-confidence signalPriced move within the discount ladder; never below margin floor
Win zone≥2 differentiators map to stated requirements and price within ±10%Lead on differentiators, hold price
Contested1 differentiator match, price within ±10%Proof-led: reference charity, pilot offer
UnknownNo signal above low confidenceAsk the discovery question; do not guess
Guardrail: the undercut verdict can propose a price, never apply one. Application always goes through the discount ladder in §9.4.

6. Routing & complexity model

Lane choice is a function of complexity, not of value alone. A £40k renewal with two stakeholders and no custom terms scores 30 and stays in assisted self-serve; a £6k first contract that arrives as a formal tender scores 55 and goes straight to enterprise. Value alone would have routed both the wrong way.

6.1 Complexity score

InputPointsTrigger
Formal procurement route40Open tender, framework call-off or DPS
Annual contract value0–300 below £5k, 15 at £5–25k, 30 above £25k
Custom requirements0–205 points each, capped at 4 requirements
Stakeholder count0–150 for ≤2, 8 for 3–4, 15 for 5+
Security / DPIA review required15Personal or beneficiary data in scope
Multi-entity or consortium10Group structure, shared service or partnership bid
Maximum130

6.2 Lane assignment

ComplexityLaneOwnership
0–24Self-serveAgent end-to-end, no human touch expected
25–54Assisted self-serveAgent-led, named rep visible and on the hook
55–130EnterpriseRep-owned, agent runs research, drafting and admin

Complexity is re-evaluated on every material change. Lanes can move in both directions: a self-serve trial that publishes a tender is promoted to enterprise; a stalled enterprise deal that shrinks in scope drops back. Every lane change writes an audit entry with the inputs that caused it.

9. Contract builder spec

The builder assembles from a governed clause library driven by the requirements register — not a template with find-and-replace. Every clause in an output document is traceable to the requirement or commercial condition that pulled it in.

9.1 Clause library schema

FieldDefinition
clause_id · versionImmutable ID with semantic version; superseded versions are retained, never edited
categoryCommercial · service · data · liability · exit · sector-specific
jurisdictionEngland & Wales · Scotland · Northern Ireland
risk_tierGreen pre-approved · Amber needs review · Red legal only
fallback_ladderPreferred → acceptable → walk-away wording, in order
triggersConditions that pull the clause in — requirement types, data classes, values
incompatible_withClause IDs that cannot co-exist; blocks assembly if both selected
approver_roleMinimum authority needed to issue this clause
owner · review_dueNamed owner and review date; expired clauses cannot be assembled

9.2 Assembly pipeline

  1. Resolve requirements — pull every register row with status met or partial.
  2. Select clauses — evaluate trigger rules; each selected clause records the req_id that caused it.
  3. Apply commercials — price, term, seats and discount from the deal record, validated against §9.4.
  4. Validate — check incompatibilities, expired versions, jurisdiction match, unresolved placeholders. Any failure blocks generation.
  5. Risk-rate the document — highest clause tier present sets the document tier, which sets the approval route.
  6. Route for approval — per the matrix in §9.5.
  7. Issue for signature — e-sign with the obligation register generated in parallel.
  8. Archive — signed PDF plus the machine-readable clause manifest and full decision trail.

10. Enrichment spec

Enrichment is what makes the personalisation credible. The failure mode is confidently stale data — a dossier that opens a conversation about a job someone left eight months ago is worse than no dossier.

Field groupRefreshStaleness actionFeeds
Org registration & financialsAnnual + on filing eventFlag at 15 monthsFit score, contract jurisdiction
Contact role & seniorityWeeklySuppress dossier at 90 daysBuying committee, dossier
Job-change signalsWeeklyExpire at 60 daysTrigger events, outreach timing
Grant & funding awardsDailyExpire at 12 monthsFunding health, intent
Tech stack fingerprintMonthlyFlag at 6 monthsFit score, competitor detection
Tender & award noticesDailyExpire at bid deadlineIntent, competitive verdict
Social & public interestsMonthlySuppress at 6 monthsConversation starters only
Product usageReal timen/aActivation, upsell, at-risk
Non-negotiable: every enriched field stores source, confidence and retrieval date, and social/interest data is used only for conversation framing — never as a scoring input. That boundary keeps the system defensible under UK GDPR and keeps reps out of territory that reads as surveillance to a charity buyer.

11. Agent autonomy & escalation

Autonomy is defined by permission tier, not by good intentions. The agent's default is to act; the tiers say how far, and the escalation table says where it stops.

11.1 Permission tiers

TierCapabilityConstraint
T0Read, enrich, score, researchAlways on, no approval
T1Send content — email, video, dossier, social touchFrequency caps and consent state respected
T2Book meetings, issue quotes, apply discountsWithin §9.4 agent authority: 10% new business, 20% retention save
T3Issue a contract for signatureGreen-tier documents in the self-serve lane only
—Never permittedPricing below margin floor, altering legal terms, safeguarding matters, formal complaints, anything red tier

11.2 Escalation triggers

TriggerThresholdHandoff
Deal valueACV above the enterprise thresholdNamed rep, agent stays as researcher
Negative sentiment2 consecutive negative-classified repliesRep within 4 working hours
Complex negotiationCounterparty edits below acceptable on the fallback ladderCommercial owner
Compliance or legalAny red-tier clause or DPIA requirementLegal, before anything is sent
Explicit requestCustomer asks for a personImmediate, no qualifying questions first
Safeguarding or complaintAny mentionImmediate to the designated human owner
Confidence floorAgent confidence below configured thresholdAsk a human rather than guess
Repeated failureSame task failed 3 timesStop, escalate, do not retry

Escalation is a handoff, not an abandonment: the agent keeps running research, drafting and admin behind the human. And every escalation is logged with its trigger — that log is the roadmap for which tier limits to relax as trust builds.

12. Gamification mechanics

The F1 framing needs real mechanics or it is decoration. Points reward the behaviour that actually compounds — fast response to hot leads and competitive wins — not raw activity volume.

12.2 Structure and safeguards

ElementDefinition
RaceOne calendar week; resets weekly so a bad week is recoverable
SeasonOne quarter; cumulative standings
Drivers' championshipIndividual reps by points
Constructors' championshipProducts by win rate — makes cross-selling a team sport
Fastest lapShortest median time to first response, published weekly

Watch for: leaderboards reliably produce the behaviour they measure. If points only reward closing, expect cherry-picking and neglected midfield accounts. The handicap and the team objective are what keep this healthy — treat them as core, not as nice-to-have.
